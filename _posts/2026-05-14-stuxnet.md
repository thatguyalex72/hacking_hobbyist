First real post here is going to be about a nasty piece of work called Stuxnet. This was a virus that originally caused a lot of concern as not a lot was known about it when it was first found. It wasn't until years later and some classified leaks later we found out its true target and what its goal was.

The beginning of this story needs some context. It's mid-2000's and Iran is on the brink of creating nuclear material. They claim it's for energy but IAEA (International Atomic Energy Agency) audits show they're using Uranium 235 enriched beyond reactor grade enrichment (around 3-5%) up to 20%. Weapons grade uranium is around 90% they were still way off but it appeared they were working towards. Now I won't dive too much into politics as that's way outside my scope, but plenty of countries had issues with this and we were actively working on delaying or even stopping that program, sometimes even using lethal means.

Now to enrich uranium (also outside my scope, but we're going to give it a shot) it’s sent through centrifuges at a specific frequency for a specific time and turned to a uranium hexafluoride gas which also gets separated into a heavier gas U-238 and the lighter U-235. Maybe I'll dive more into this exact process later, but the key component for this story is they use centrifuges. These are essentially computer controller spinning tubes of metal that can spin very fast in some cases.

During this time George W Bush is president of the US and wants to find another more effective way to stop Iran's enrichment. Iran had just finished construction on the Natanz enrichment facility. They had the latest computer controlled machines (OT = Operation Technology as opposed to IT, Information Technology) and contractors in and out of the facility daily for updates, fixes, general maintenance. Someone had an idea to use a contractor to sneak a virus into the facility.

OT environments are usually air-gapped meaning they do not connect to the outside world or public internet, so the virus wouldn't have a way to reach out and see what commands or instructions were available, so it had to act completely autonomously once deployed. This means it needed to have worming capibilities (lateral movement from machine to machine) and its payload needed to be effective enough to do its job but stealthy enough to remain undetected. This virus, once popularized and analyzed was named Stuxnet. It's payload would infect PLCs (Programmable Logic Controllers) that connected to these centrifuges. They would sit dormant for up to 2 weeks collecting normal data and learning how normal operations went. Once the payload was enacted, it would rapidly change the centrifuge speeds which would eventually break the machines. One of the more clever pieces of this malware was it would feed normal data back to the computers so the technicians could hear and see the machines getting destroyed but the computers kept saying things are normal. The chef's kiss here is that it would intercept any emergency shutoff signals and keep the destruction of the machines going. This had to cause chaos and confusion to the operators.

Now this went on for a while and was very effective. However one oversight was that there was no kill switch or no way to stop this virus from spreading or enacting its payload on targets. It was smart to recognize PLC’s that had specific serial numbers to the location it was supposed to attack, so when it eventually made its way out into the world and infected machines that weren’t its intended target Stuxnet would just sit there doing nothing. It didn’t take long after that for security researchers to find this software and start sharing samples of it. Researchers found that it was incredibly high quality code, meaning little to no bugs. Every piece of code had a specific purpose and it was written without any excess lines or errors. It also leveraged multiple zero day vulnerabilities, where more pieces of malware might have one. 

A zero day is a vulnerability no one knows about, not even the software vendor. It has zero days protection and no available updates to fix the vulnerability. These kinds of bugs are very valuable not only in the bug bounty community but also to malicious threat actors. 

So people realized this malware was bad but had no way to really protect against, however it wasn’t attacking anything we were really aware of, which lead to more confusion. It took years of research and disclosures to eventually realize this malware was created by the US government who also partnered with the Israeli government to stop nuclear enrichment in Iran.

Now something that stood out to me while researching this and watching an Alex Gibney documentary called “Zero Days” is that this program legally had to be renewed every 90 days. The authorization for this program needed to come from the president of the US and at this time the only other weapon the US had in its arsenal that required that level of authorization were nuclear weapons. 

This is one of the first examples in history of software causing physical damage, and that potential was known well enough that the US government needed to go to the highest level for authorization to use it in the field. The unfortunate part of all of this is that Stuxnet (or Olympic Games, its internal project name) was never officially claimed or attributed to anyone, so everything we know about this legendary piece of malware is rumors and leaks.
